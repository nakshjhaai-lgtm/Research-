## What was built

Three **independent** implementations of the same physical quantity, so they can
be checked against each other rather than against a single formula:

| # | Function | Method |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `pc2d` (`src/pc2d.py`) | Chan 2008 small-HBR **closed form** |
| 2 | `pc2d_1d` (`src/foster2d.py`) | Eq. **N-2** 1-D error-function reduction (Alfano 2005a / Elrod 2019) |
| 3 | `pc2d_numeric` (`src/pc2d.py`) | Foster & Estes 1992 **2-D quadrature** of Eq. **N-1** |
| 4 | `cara_monte_carlo` (`src/foster2d.py`) | **6-D** state draw → propagate → encounter plane → count hits |

**Attribution, corrected after reading the source.** Appendix N says the
conjunction-plane method "was developed for the Space Shuttle Program and first
described in the literature in 1992 (Foster and Estes)" and that "Foster and
Estes (1992) applied a **two-dimensional quadrature** technique". So *Foster* is
the 2-D quadrature (`pc2d_numeric`), **not** the 1-D erf form. The 1-D form is
Eq. N-2, credited to Alfano (2005a) and refined by Elrod (2019), who uses erfc
differences and Gauss–Chebyshev quadrature for accuracy at small Pc. An earlier
version of this note attributed the 1-D form to Foster; that was wrong.
`foster2d` remains as an alias of `pc2d_1d` so existing scripts run.

`pc2d_1d` uses Gauss–Legendre rather than Elrod's Gauss–Chebyshev; both converge
on the same integral, and the 200-node result matches the exact Rayleigh CDF to
6.5e-08 (Check 1 below), so the choice is one of efficiency, not correctness.

Why `pc2d_1d` is a genuinely independent path: `pc2d_numeric` integrates in
polar coordinates with a trapezoid on `ρ dρ`. `pc2d_1d` rotates into the
covariance principal axes, does the *inner* integral **exactly** with error
functions, and quadratures only the outer coordinate — this is Eq. N-2 verbatim:

```
Pc = ∫₋ᴿᴿ φ(x; m₁′, s₁) · [Φ((h(x)−m₂′)/s₂) − Φ((−h(x)−m₂′)/s₂)] dx,   h(x)=√(R²−x²)
```

Agreement between the two is therefore a real check on both, not a tautology.

### The authoritative source, now actually read

`notes/learning_path.md` previously cited the CARA Best Practices Handbook
Appendix N **from prior knowledge, without fetching it**. It has now been fetched
and read (13 chunks). What it establishes, and what was wrong before:

- **Eq. N-1** is the 2-D conjunction-plane Pc integral — exactly what
  `pc2d_numeric` computes.
- **Eq. N-2** is the 1-D erf form — exactly what `pc2d_1d` computes.
- **Eqs. N-3/N-4** give `P_sq`, the circumscribing-square upper bound, and note
  that *"CDMs generated by the USSPACECOM ASW processing system often report
  P_sq as the estimate of conjunction's collision probability."* The Kelvins
  `risk` field is **not** P_sq — it is the disk form, verified to 0.0012 dex.
- **Eqs. N-7/N-8** give multi-encounter `Nc = Σ Nc,k` and the Pc bounds.
  (The notes previously cited a nonexistent "eq. N-23" for this.)
- **Eq. N-10** gives the decorrelated joint covariance for cross-correlated
  drag error. (Previously cited as a nonexistent "eq. N-18".)

### CARA's own validation of 2-D Pc — the benchmark to beat

Appendix N Figure N-7 compares 2-D Pc against high-fidelity Monte Carlo on
**63,603 real CARA conjunctions** (2017-05-01 to 2019-08-15, 2-D Pc > 1e-7):

| 2-D Pc vs Monte Carlo | conjunctions | share |
|---|---|---|
| agree (p > 1e-3) | 62,913 | 98.915% |
| disagree (p ≤ 1e-3) | 254 | 0.399% |
| disagree (p ≤ 1e-6) | 436 | 0.686% |

**~1.1% of real conjunctions violate the conjunction-plane assumptions**, and
Appendix N attributes those failures to violations of its four stated
assumptions: statistical independence, rectilinear motion, Gaussian positions
with negligible velocity uncertainty, and temporal isolation. The 2-D and 3-D Nc
methods match the Monte Carlo far better (66 yellow, 0 red).

This is the number to hold any 2-D implementation against — and it is a
*population* failure rate, which is exactly the kind of quantity a single-case
comparison cannot give.

`cara_monte_carlo` is the only one of the three that sees the
**position–velocity covariance block** — the block that is present in
`raw_data_2015-2019.gz` but absent from the flattened competition CSVs, and the
likely cause of the unexplained χ² ≥ 10 residual in `notes/learning_path.md` §4.5.

## The finding worth reporting: the small-HBR approximation is not free

Check 2b sweeps R/σ against the ellipse eccentricity. The Chan closed form
deviates from the exact integral by:

| R/σ | ecc = 1 | ecc = 3 | ecc = 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.02 | +0.01% | +0.05% | +0.51% |
| 0.05 | +0.06% | +0.31% | **+3.16%** |
| 0.10 | +0.25% | +1.25% | **+12.62%** |
| 0.20 | +1.00% | +5.03% | **+49.28%** |
| 0.40 | +4.05% | +20.40% | **+165.76%** |
| 0.80 | +16.85% | +83.66% | **+458.86%** |

The universally-used operational formula is only safe when **R ≪ σ *and* the
ellipse is not too elongated**. At R/σ = 0.2 with eccentricity 10 — entirely
ordinary for a LEO conjunction a few days out, where the along-track sigma is
routinely 5–10× the radial — the closed form is **49% high**. It always errs in
the same direction (high), because replacing the integrand by its value at the
centre of the disk over-weights the peak.

`pc2d_1d` has no such approximation and costs one 200-node Gauss–Legendre
quadrature.

**CARA documents the same limit from the other direction.** Appendix N gives
`R_lim = 4σ_x − min(R)`: Gauss–Chebyshev quadrature "becomes increasingly
inaccurate as the hard-body radius grows beyond R_lim", and an adaptive
integrator must be used instead. So the numerical schemes have their own domain
limit at large R/σ, independent of the small-HBR approximation's limit at the
same end of the range. Both point the same way: **the conjunction-plane 2-D
method is a small-HBR method, and R/σ ≳ 0.1 needs care.**

## Validation against CARA tools — DONE, and it changes the headline

**The standing "CARA is a licensed NASA-internal product" claim was wrong.**
`nasa/CARA_Analysis_Tools` is **public** on GitHub under the NASA Open Source
Software Agreement (NOSA GSC-18593-1 / 18848-1 / 19027-1 / 19374-1), last
updated 8 September 2026, and it ships the entire probability-of-collision SDK:
`Pc2D_Foster.m`, `PcElrod.m`, `PcCircle.m`, `Pc2D_Hall.m`, `Pc3D_Hall.m`,
`PcMultiStep.m`, `UsageViolationPc2D.m`, plus `Utils/CovRemEigValClip.m` and the
Alfano (2009) test cases **with their published expected values**. It clones and
runs under GNU Octave 9.4.0 (`quad2d` is available; only the pre-compiled
`SDMC_Utils` MEX binaries are unusable).

### What was run

`scripts/extract_alfano_cases.py` pulls the 12 cases out of CARA's own
`.xls` inputs (Octave's `io` package cannot read legacy `.xls`, so the numbers
are extracted in Python and handed to CARA's code). `scripts/run_cara.m` then
runs **CARA's actual `Pc2D_Foster.m`** on them and writes
`/tmp/cara_reference.csv`; `scripts/validate_against_cara.py` runs *our*
implementations on the identical inputs.

**CARA's own code reproduces its published values to 1e-12 relative** — so the
Octave environment genuinely is CARA, not a re-implementation of it.

### Results — ours vs CARA's `Pc2D_Foster` on the published Alfano cases

| method | worst rel err | median rel err | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`pc2d_1d` (Eq. N-2, Alfano 2005a / Elrod 2019)** | **8.17e-07** | 6.74e-08 | agrees |
| **`pc2d_numeric` (Foster & Estes 1992 2-D quadrature)** | **6.89e-06** | 4.11e-07 | agrees |
| `cara_monte_carlo` (CARA-style 6-D MC, 2M samples) | worst \|z\| = **3.10** | — | agrees within 4σ |
| `pc2d` (Alfriend–Akella / Chan small-HBR closed form) | **1.21e+01** | **1.00e+00** | **wrong by up to 13×** |

CARA's own unit test asserts `RelTol = 1e-3`. Our 1-D erf reduction is
**~1200× tighter than CARA's own acceptance tolerance** on the same cases.

### The finding that matters: the closed form is not merely imprecise here

`pc2d_max = R²/(2·√det C)` is what the small-HBR closed form returns at **zero
miss distance**. On CARA's own published cases it is already **greater than 1**
for cases 4 (3.51), 5 (1.01), 9 and 10 (4.86) — impossible for a probability,
before the exponential term acts at all. At non-zero miss the `exp(−χ²/2)`
factor then drives it to **1.4e-25 against CARA's 4.93e-02** (case 4): a
**24-order-of-magnitude** error, because the disk centre sits ~10σ out along the
minor axis while the disk is still wide enough to straddle the ridge of a highly
elongated covariance.

This is Appendix N's own limit, `R_lim = 4σ_x − min(R)`, observed in the
published cases rather than derived. The earlier synthetic sweep found +12.6%
at R/σ = 0.1 and +458.9% at 0.8; **these cases are simply much worse than
that**, and they are the cases CARA publishes.

**Practical consequence: do not use the small-HBR closed form on real
conjunctions. Use Eq. N-2, which is both exact and fast.**

### The ESA anchor still stands

`scripts/validate_pipeline.py` check 3 reproduces ESA's operational `risk`
field to a median error of **+0.0012 dex** (55.9% of rows within 0.05 dex) over
199,082 CDMs. That is a *second*, independent, operational confirmation of the
same quantity — now joined by a first-party one from CARA.

Reproducing the CARA comparison needs the clone and Octave:
```
git clone https://github.com/nasa/CARA_Analysis_Tools.git
sudo apt-get install -y --no-install-recommends octave octave-io
python3 scripts/extract_alfano_cases.py
octave --no-gui --quiet scripts/run_cara.m

```

## HBR variants

`src/foster2d.py` implements the three HBR conventions as functions:

- `hbr_flat_5m()` — the Space-Track / 18 SPCS EVR convention
- `hbr_catalogue(t_span, c_span)` — `(t_span + c_span)/2`, what ESA uses here
- `hbr_rcs_uncertainty(...)` — radius treated as uncertain, inflating E[R²] by
  `1 + σ_rel²`

**The HBR in this dataset is not 5 m.** The catalogue HBR ranges 1.05–18.35 m
(median 5.55 m). Using a flat 5 m is a *different assumption*, not a variant of
the same one — and `scripts/attribution_199k.py` Table E quantifies what that
assumption costs.
